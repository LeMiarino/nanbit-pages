Nanbit Support

Nanbit is made by Denver Delorino.

Nanbit is a habit tracker. You add a few habits, pick which days each one is due, and tick them off. It keeps your streaks, shows a calendar of what you have done, and reminds you if you ask it to. Nanbit keeps all of it on your phone and sends none of it anywhere.

Need help? Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com.

It reaches one person, so a reply normally takes a few days. It helps to include your phone model, its operating-system version, and the version and commit shown under You → About Nanbit — that last one identifies exactly which build you are running, which two identical-looking version numbers cannot.

Questions people ask first

Where is my data, and what happens if I lose my phone?

On the phone. Nanbit has no account and no server, which means there is nothing to sign into and nothing for Nanbit to restore from. If you want a copy, use You → Export data — it writes a JSON or CSV file and hands it to the share sheet, so you can put it wherever you keep things. That export is free whether or not you subscribe.

Your phone's own backup can hold a copy too; the privacy policy says what is known about that on each platform. If a device is lost with no export and no backup of the phone, the habits on it are gone. That is the honest cost of an app that keeps nothing about you.

Can I move my habits to a new phone?

Export from the old phone, then use You → Export data → Import on the new one. That is the route to rely on, because it is the one Nanbit does itself. On iPhone your phone's own backup can include Nanbit's database as well — Apple documents that iCloud Backup covers the data of apps you have downloaded, and Nanbit keeps its database where that applies — so restoring a backup onto the new phone would be expected to bring your habits with it. Nobody has tested a restore, so if it matters, export.

I subscribed, but Nanbit says I am on the free tier.

Open You → Nanbit Pro → Restore purchases. A subscription attaches to the Apple Account that bought it, so a restore is all that is needed on a reinstall or a new device. There is no sign-in, because there is nothing to sign into. If that does not fix it, check you are signed into the same Apple Account that made the purchase.

On Android, open that screen and read it first. While it says Nanbit Pro is not on sale yet, nothing can have been bought through Nanbit on that phone, so a restore has nothing to find and will tell you so. Everything free stays free.

What does Pro actually unlock?

Unlimited habits instead of five, every range on the Stats tab instead of the week, and the whole of History — opening any past day, and paging back through earlier months. None of that changes what Nanbit keeps: nothing you have tracked is deleted or hidden by not subscribing, and it all goes on counting toward your streaks and totals. What Pro buys is the view of it. Without Pro you can open and change today and see this month's shape, while opening a past day or an earlier month is what asks you to subscribe — and You → Export data is free either way. Nothing you made is taken away if a subscription lapses: every habit stays, and only a new one is refused.

My reminders are not arriving.

Three things to check, in order:

  • Your phone's own notification settings — on iPhone, Settings → Notifications → Nanbit, where a Focus mode or Scheduled Summary can also hold reminders back. On Android, Settings → Apps → Nanbit → Notifications, where the Reminders channel has a switch of its own and Do Not Disturb can silence it; the exact path varies by manufacturer.
  • You → Reminders in Nanbit — the daily reminder has its own switch, and each habit has one of its own underneath.
  • A reminder for a habit you have already ticked is skipped on purpose. That is the intended behaviour, not a fault.

Why does a habit's name appear on my lock screen?

Because a reminder names the habit it is for. That is your phone displaying a notification it scheduled itself — nothing is sent anywhere. To keep names off the lock screen, use Settings → Notifications → Show Previews on iPhone, or your Android phone's lock-screen notification setting.

I archived a habit. Why does it still count toward the free limit?

The free limit is five habits held, archived ones included — archiving keeps a habit's history rather than removing it. You → Archived habits lets you restore one or delete it outright, and deleting frees the slot.

Can I edit a day I forgot to tick?

Yes, for the last week. Open the day on the History tab and tick the habits there. Days older than a week are shown but cannot be changed, so a streak cannot be quietly rewritten months later.

How do I delete everything?

You → About Nanbit → Reset all data erases Nanbit's database — every habit, every completion, your settings, and the records you had already deleted. It stops a little short of a fresh install: a couple of small diagnostic files Nanbit keeps for itself are left behind, and your phone does not forget that you once allowed notifications. Removing the app clears more than that, because it takes everything Nanbit ever wrote. Neither reaches a backup your phone has already made.

How do I cancel a subscription?

In the store that sold it. On iPhone, open the Settings app, tap your name, then tap Subscriptions. On Android, go to subscriptions in Google Play. Cancelling never deletes anything you have tracked.

Reporting a bug

Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com with what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. The commit shown under You → About Nanbit is the single most useful line to include: two different builds can carry the same version number, and the commit tells them apart.

Privacy

Nanbit stores your habits on your device, has no accounts, and runs no analytics about how you use the app. The full account of what is stored and the few things that leave the phone is in the privacy policy.
