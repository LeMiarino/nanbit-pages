Nanbit Support

Nanbit is made by Denver Delorino.

Nanbit is a habit tracker. You add a few habits, pick which days each one is due, and tick them off. It keeps your streaks, shows a calendar of what you have done, and reminds you if you ask it to. There is no account: your habits are kept on your phone, and Nanbit never sends them anywhere.

Need help? Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com.

It reaches one person, so a reply normally takes a few days. It helps to include your phone model, its operating-system version, and the version and commit shown under You → About Nanbit — that last one identifies exactly which build you are running, which two identical-looking version numbers cannot.

Questions people ask first

Where is my data, and what happens if I lose my phone?

Your habits are kept on the phone, and Nanbit never sends them anywhere. Your phone's own backup can hold a copy; the privacy policy says what is known about that on each platform. If the phone is lost and there is no backup of it, the habits on it are gone.

Can I move my habits to a new phone?

On iPhone, set the new phone up from your old one, or from its iCloud backup, and your habits come along with your other apps' data. Apple says iCloud Backup "includes app data for the apps that you've downloaded on your device", and Nanbit keeps its database in the app's own storage, with nothing keeping it out of a backup or a transfer. We have not yet watched this happen with Nanbit, so check your habits on the new phone before you erase the old one.

I subscribed, but Nanbit says I am on the free tier.

Open You → Nanbit Pro → Restore purchases. A subscription attaches to the Apple Account that bought it, so a restore is all that is needed on a reinstall or a new device. There is nothing to sign in to in Nanbit: a subscription belongs to your Apple Account. If that does not fix it, check you are signed into the same Apple Account that made the purchase.

On Android, open that screen and read it first. While it says Nanbit Pro is not on sale yet, nothing can have been bought through Nanbit on that phone, so a restore has nothing to find and will tell you so. Everything free stays free.

What does Pro actually unlock?

Unlimited habits instead of five, every range on the Stats tab instead of the week, and the whole of History — opening any past day, and paging back through earlier months. None of that changes what Nanbit keeps: nothing you have tracked is deleted or hidden by not subscribing, and it all goes on counting toward your streaks and totals. What Pro buys is the view of it. Without Pro you can open and change today and see this month's shape, while opening a past day or an earlier month is what asks you to subscribe. Nothing you made is taken away if a subscription lapses: every habit stays, and only a new one is refused.

My reminders are not arriving.

Three things to check, in order:

  • Your phone's own notification settings — on iPhone, Settings → Notifications → Nanbit, where a Focus mode or Scheduled Summary can also hold reminders back. On Android, Settings → Apps → Nanbit → Notifications, where the Reminders channel has a switch of its own and Do Not Disturb can silence it; the exact path varies by manufacturer.
  • You → Reminders in Nanbit — the daily reminder has its own switch, and each habit has one of its own underneath.
  • A reminder for a habit you have already ticked is skipped on purpose. That is the intended behaviour, not a fault.

Why does a habit's name appear on my lock screen?

Because a reminder names the habit it is for. That is your phone displaying a notification it scheduled itself — nothing is sent anywhere. To keep names off the lock screen, use Settings → Notifications → Show Previews on iPhone, or your Android phone's lock-screen notification setting.

What happens when I delete a habit?

It comes off Today and the widget, and its reminder stops if it has one. Its past days stay in History and Stats, marked Deleted, and can no longer be ticked. A deleted habit does not count toward the free limit of five, so deleting one makes room for another. Deleting cannot be undone.

Can I edit a day I forgot to tick?

Yes, for the last week. Open the day on the History tab and tick the habits there. Days older than a week are shown but cannot be changed, so a streak cannot be quietly rewritten months later.

How do I delete everything?

You → About Nanbit → Reset all data erases Nanbit's database — every habit, every completion, your settings, and the records you had already deleted — and on iPhone empties the copy Nanbit keeps for its widget. It cannot be undone. It stops a little short of a fresh install: a couple of small diagnostic files Nanbit keeps for itself are left behind, and your phone does not forget that you once allowed notifications.

Removing the app clears more than that, the widget's copy included. Neither reaches a backup your phone has already made.

How do I cancel a subscription?

In the store that sold it. On iPhone, open the Settings app, tap your name, then tap Subscriptions. On Android, go to subscriptions in Google Play. Cancelling never deletes anything you have tracked.

Reporting a bug

Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com with what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. The commit shown under You → About Nanbit is the single most useful line to include: two different builds can carry the same version number, and the commit tells them apart.

Privacy

Nanbit runs no analytics about how you use the app, and has no account. It keeps your habits on your phone and never sends them anywhere. Everything that is stored, and the few things that leave the phone, are in the privacy policy.
