Nanbit Support

Nanbit is made by Denver Delorino.

Nanbit is a habit tracker. You add a few habits, pick which days each one is due, and tick them off. It keeps your streaks, shows a calendar of what you have done, and reminds you if you ask it to. You can use it without an account. On iPhone you can also sign in with Apple, and Nanbit backs up your habits to your account.

Need help? Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com.

It reaches one person, so a reply normally takes a few days. It helps to include your phone model, its operating-system version, and the version and commit shown under You → About Nanbit — that last one identifies exactly which build you are running, which two identical-looking version numbers cannot.

Questions people ask first

Where is my data, and what happens if I lose my phone?

If you have not signed in, your habits are kept on the phone, and Nanbit does not send them anywhere. Your phone's own backup can hold a copy; the privacy policy says what is known about that on each platform. If the phone is lost and there is no backup of it, the habits on it are gone.

If you signed in with Apple on iPhone, your habits are also backed up to your account. Sign in on your new iPhone and they come back. The backup is kept by Supabase, the company that runs Nanbit's server, in Singapore. The privacy policy has the details.

Can I move my habits to a new phone?

On iPhone, sign in with Apple. Sign in under You on the old phone, then on the new one, and your habits come back on the new phone. Your account works on one phone at a time, so signing in on the new one signs the old one out and takes the habits off it. They stay in your account.

Your phone's own backup should carry your habits across as well. Apple says iCloud Backup "includes app data for the apps that you've downloaded on your device", and Nanbit keeps its database in the app's own storage on the phone rather than in iCloud Drive. Both of those come from Apple's documentation and from reading the code, not from restoring a phone, and Nanbit has not been tested with a restore — so if it matters, sign in.

I subscribed, but Nanbit says I am on the free tier.

Open You → Nanbit Pro → Restore purchases. A subscription attaches to the Apple Account that bought it, so a restore is all that is needed on a reinstall or a new device. You do not need to sign in to Nanbit for this: a subscription belongs to your Apple Account, not to your Nanbit account. If that does not fix it, check you are signed into the same Apple Account that made the purchase.

On Android, open that screen and read it first. While it says Nanbit Pro is not on sale yet, nothing can have been bought through Nanbit on that phone, so a restore has nothing to find and will tell you so. Everything free stays free.

What does Pro actually unlock?

Unlimited habits instead of five, every range on the Stats tab instead of the week, and the whole of History — opening any past day, and paging back through earlier months. None of that changes what Nanbit keeps: nothing you have tracked is deleted or hidden by not subscribing, and it all goes on counting toward your streaks and totals. What Pro buys is the view of it. Without Pro you can open and change today and see this month's shape, while opening a past day or an earlier month is what asks you to subscribe. Signing in and the account backup are free either way. Nothing you made is taken away if a subscription lapses: every habit stays, and only a new one is refused.

My reminders are not arriving.

Three things to check, in order:

  • Your phone's own notification settings — on iPhone, Settings → Notifications → Nanbit, where a Focus mode or Scheduled Summary can also hold reminders back. On Android, Settings → Apps → Nanbit → Notifications, where the Reminders channel has a switch of its own and Do Not Disturb can silence it; the exact path varies by manufacturer.
  • You → Reminders in Nanbit — the daily reminder has its own switch, and each habit has one of its own underneath.
  • A reminder for a habit you have already ticked is skipped on purpose. That is the intended behaviour, not a fault.

Why does a habit's name appear on my lock screen?

Because a reminder names the habit it is for. That is your phone displaying a notification it scheduled itself — nothing is sent anywhere. To keep names off the lock screen, use Settings → Notifications → Show Previews on iPhone, or your Android phone's lock-screen notification setting.

I archived a habit. Why does it still count toward the free limit?

The free limit is five habits held, archived ones included — archiving keeps a habit's history rather than removing it. You → Archived habits lets you restore one or delete it outright, and deleting frees the slot.

Can I edit a day I forgot to tick?

Yes, for the last week. Open the day on the History tab and tick the habits there. Days older than a week are shown but cannot be changed, so a streak cannot be quietly rewritten months later.

How do I delete everything?

If you are signed in, use You → Delete account. It deletes your account and its backup from Nanbit's server, then takes everything off the phone. It needs an internet connection, and it cannot be undone. To also remove Nanbit from the apps that use your Apple Account, open the Settings app, tap your name, tap Sign in with Apple, choose Nanbit, then tap Delete.

If you are not signed in, You → About Nanbit → Reset all data erases Nanbit's database — every habit, every completion, your settings, and the records you had already deleted. It stops a little short of a fresh install: a couple of small diagnostic files Nanbit keeps for itself are left behind, and your phone does not forget that you once allowed notifications.

Removing the app clears more than that. It does not delete your account, and on iPhone it leaves the sign-in in the phone's Keychain, which iOS keeps after an app is removed; Nanbit clears it the next time it is installed and opened. None of these reaches a backup your phone has already made.

How do I cancel a subscription?

In the store that sold it. On iPhone, open the Settings app, tap your name, then tap Subscriptions. On Android, go to subscriptions in Google Play. Cancelling never deletes anything you have tracked.

Reporting a bug

Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com with what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. The commit shown under You → About Nanbit is the single most useful line to include: two different builds can carry the same version number, and the commit tells them apart.

Privacy

Nanbit runs no analytics about how you use the app. Without an account it keeps your habits on your device. If you sign in with Apple on iPhone, they are backed up to your account. Everything that is stored, and everything that leaves the phone, is in the privacy policy.
