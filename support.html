Nanbit Support

Nanbit is made by Denver Delorino.

Nanbit is a habit tracker for iPhone. You add a few habits, pick which days each one is due, and tick them off. It keeps your streaks, shows a calendar of what you have done, and reminds you if you ask it to. Everything stays on your phone.

Need help? Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com.

It reaches one person, so a reply normally takes a few days. It helps to include your iPhone model, your iOS version, and the version and commit shown under You → About Nanbit — that last one identifies exactly which build you are running, which two identical-looking version numbers cannot.

Questions people ask first

Where is my data, and what happens if I lose my phone?

On the phone, and only there. Nanbit has no account and no server, which means there is nothing to sign into and nothing to restore from. If you want a copy, use You → Export data — it writes a JSON or CSV file and hands it to the share sheet, so you can put it wherever you keep things. That export is free whether or not you subscribe.

If a device is lost without an export, the habits on it are gone. That is the honest cost of an app that keeps nothing about you.

Can I move my habits to a new phone?

Export from the old phone, then use You → Export data → Import on the new one. A restored iPhone backup carries Nanbit's database across as well.

I subscribed, but Nanbit says I am on the free tier.

Open You → Nanbit Pro → Restore purchases. Subscriptions attach to the Apple Account that bought them, so a restore is all that is needed on a reinstall or a new device — no sign-in, because there is nothing to sign into. If that does not fix it, check you are signed into the same Apple Account that made the purchase.

What does Pro actually unlock?

Unlimited habits instead of five, every range on the Stats tab instead of the week, and the whole of History — opening any past day, and paging back through earlier months. Everything you have already tracked stays visible and editable whether or not you subscribe, and nothing you made is ever taken away if a subscription lapses.

My reminders are not arriving.

Three things to check, in order:

  • iOS Settings → Notifications → Nanbit — notifications have to be allowed there, and a Focus mode or Scheduled Summary can hold them back.
  • You → Reminders in Nanbit — the daily reminder has its own switch, and each habit has one of its own underneath.
  • A reminder for a habit you have already ticked is skipped on purpose. That is the intended behaviour, not a fault.

Why does a habit's name appear on my lock screen?

Because a reminder names the habit it is for. That is your phone displaying a notification it scheduled itself — nothing is sent anywhere. To keep names off the lock screen, use iOS Settings → Notifications → Show Previews.

I archived a habit. Why does it still count toward the free limit?

The free limit is five habits held, archived ones included — archiving keeps a habit's history rather than removing it. You → Archived habits lets you restore one or delete it outright, and deleting frees the slot.

Can I edit a day I forgot to tick?

Yes, for the last week. Open the day on the History tab and tick the habits there. Days older than a week are shown but cannot be changed, so a streak cannot be quietly rewritten months later.

How do I delete everything?

You → About Nanbit → Reset all data returns the app to a fresh install and erases the database, including records you had already deleted. Removing the app does the same thing.

How do I cancel a subscription?

In iOS Settings → your name → Subscriptions, which is where Apple keeps every subscription. Cancelling never deletes anything you have tracked.

Reporting a bug

Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com with what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. The commit shown under You → About Nanbit is the single most useful line to include: two different builds can carry the same version number, and the commit tells them apart.

Privacy

Nanbit stores your habits on your device, has no accounts, and runs no analytics about how you use the app. The full account of what is stored and the few things that leave the phone is in the privacy policy.
