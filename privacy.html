Nanbit Privacy Policy

Effective 13 September 2026. Nanbit is made by Denver Delorino, an individual developer.

Nanbit is a habit tracker. You can use it without an account. On iPhone you can also sign in with Apple, and then Nanbit backs up your habits to your account so you can get them back. This policy explains what is kept on your phone, what leaves it, where it goes, and how to delete it.

The short version

  • Without an account, your habits, your completions and your settings are kept on your device, and Nanbit does not send them anywhere. If your phone backs itself up, that backup can include them — see section 5 below.
  • On iPhone you can sign in with Apple. If you do, Nanbit backs up your habits, your completions and your settings to your account. The backup is kept by Supabase, the company that runs Nanbit's server, in Singapore. On Android there is no sign-in.
  • Signing out takes your habits off the phone and leaves them in your account. Deleting your account deletes them from the account and from the phone.
  • Nanbit has no analytics about how you use the app, no advertising, no tracking, and no crash-reporting service.
  • Nanbit uses a service called RevenueCat to manage subscriptions. In a version of Nanbit configured to sell them, its software runs whenever the app is open, so a small request goes to RevenueCat when Nanbit starts, whether or not you ever subscribe. It carries a random identifier for the installation and nothing about your habits. A version that is not configured to sell subscriptions does not contact RevenueCat at all.
  • If you buy a Nanbit subscription, the store you buy it through processes the payment: Apple on iPhone, Google Play on Android.
  • If the app crashes, the next launch may ask Expo's update service for a corrected version of its own code, and that request includes the text of the error. It does not check at any other time.
  • Nanbit does not sell or share your data.

What Nanbit stores on your device

All of this lives in a database inside the app's own storage, which only Nanbit can read:

  • Habits — the name, icon, colour, schedule, daily goal and reminder time you set.
  • Completions — which habit you marked done, on which day, and how many times.
  • Settings — your display name, appearance choice, which day your week starts on, the streak grace-day switch, your reminder preferences, and some bookkeeping such as whether you have finished the introduction. Two more fields were set aside for accounts and are always empty; the account does not use them. The display name is whatever you type, or the name Apple gives Nanbit when you first sign in (section 1).
  • A summary cache — daily totals worked out from the above, kept so the charts are fast. It contains nothing new.
  • Records you have deleted. When you delete a habit or clear a day, Nanbit marks the record as deleted and hides it everywhere in the app, but the row stays in the database file. It is never sent to your account, though a backup your phone makes copies the database whole, deleted records included. Reset all data, signing out and deleting your account each remove it along with everything else.
  • If you sign in, two more things, kept in the iPhone's Keychain rather than the database: the sign-in that lets Nanbit reach your account, and the identifier Apple gives Nanbit for you. Signing out and deleting your account remove both.

A copy for the widget, on iPhone. Nanbit also keeps a small copy for its home-screen widget, even if you never add the widget. It is outside the database, in storage that only Nanbit and its widget can read. It says which habits are due and which are done, today and for the next week, as marks and totals, without the habits' names. Signing out, deleting your account and Reset all data each replace it with one that holds no habits. If that replacement fails, the widget can go on showing the last week it was given until Nanbit next updates it.

Unless you sign in, Nanbit sends none of this anywhere. Deleting the app removes the database from the phone, but not from a backup the phone has already made — see section 5. Apple says that iOS removes the storage an app shares with its widget when the app is removed. On iPhone, iOS keeps Keychain items after an app is removed, so a sign-in can be left there; Nanbit clears it the next time it is installed and opened, without contacting the server.

What leaves your device

Nanbit itself sends four things, and nothing else. Only the first needs you to sign in. A fifth is sent by your phone rather than by Nanbit: its own backup, described last.

1. Your account and its backup, if you sign in

This happens only on iPhone, and only if you sign in. Nothing in Nanbit needs an account, and buying or restoring a subscription never needs one. If you never sign in, nothing in this section happens.

Signing in. Nanbit uses Sign in with Apple. Apple asks you whether to share your name and your email address. If you choose Hide My Email, Apple gives Nanbit a random address that forwards to yours, instead of your own. Apple then gives Nanbit a sign-in token, and Nanbit sends that token to Supabase, which creates your account.

What your account holds:

  • The email address Apple gave Nanbit — your own, or the forwarding address if you chose Hide My Email.
  • The identifier Apple gives Nanbit for you. It is not your Apple Account.
  • The backup: your habits, archived ones included, your completions, and your settings, including your display name, plus the time it was saved. It does not include the records you have deleted, or the summary cache.
  • A record of each sign-in: when it happened, the IP address it came from, and what kind of device and app made it.

Your name is not part of your account. Apple sends it once, the first time you sign in. If you have not chosen a name in Nanbit, Nanbit puts it on the You tab. From then on it is one of your settings, and goes into the backup with the rest of them.

When it is sent. While you are signed in, Nanbit saves the backup a couple of seconds after you change something, and again when you leave the app. If nothing has changed, nothing is sent. When you sign in, Nanbit fetches the backup your account already has. And when you open Nanbit, it checks at most once a minute that you are still signed in, and your iPhone asks Apple whether you still allow Nanbit to use Sign in with Apple.

Where it goes, and how. Everything travels over an encrypted connection. It is kept by Supabase (Supabase Pte. Ltd.), which runs Nanbit's server, on servers in Singapore. The backup is stored as Nanbit writes it. It is not encrypted in a way that stops Supabase or Nanbit's developer from reading it. Supabase handles all of it for Nanbit under its data processing agreement.

Supabase's logs. Supabase keeps logs of the requests Nanbit makes to its server. They include your IP address, the rough location it points to, such as your city, what kind of device and app made the request, and, when you sign in, your email address. Supabase says it keeps these logs for up to 7 days, so anything in them that deleting your account cannot reach is gone on its own within that time.

Two other things are Supabase's, and it does not say how long it keeps them. It makes daily backup copies of Nanbit's database, so a deleted account can stay in those copies for a while. And it keeps records of connections to its own systems, for security.

One phone at a time. Your account works on one phone at a time. Signing in on another phone signs the first one out, and takes the habits off it. They stay in your account.

Signing out takes your habits off the phone, along with its reminders and the widget's copy. They stay in your account, and signing in again brings them back.

Deleting your account is at You → Delete account. It needs an internet connection. It deletes your account, the backup and the sign-in records from Supabase first, and then takes everything off the phone. It cannot be undone. If Nanbit cannot reach Supabase, nothing is deleted and nothing changes on the phone. It cannot reach a backup your phone made earlier (section 5), or Supabase's logs, backup copies and security records, described above.

Deleting your account does not remove Nanbit from the apps that use your Apple Account. To do that too, open the Settings app, tap your name, tap Sign in with Apple, choose Nanbit, then tap Delete. If you skip this and sign in again later, Apple will not ask to share your name a second time, so Nanbit will not get it.

2. Subscriptions, and the app knowing whether you have one

Nanbit uses RevenueCat to tell whether a subscription is active. Whether it contacts RevenueCat at all is settled when a version of the app is built: a version configured to sell subscriptions contacts it, and a version that is not does not contact it at all — not at launch, not when you open the subscription screen, not at any other time.

  • Every time you open Nanbit, and every time you return to it, the app asks RevenueCat what this installation is entitled to. This happens whether or not you have ever subscribed, and whether or not you have ever opened the subscription screen. What is sent is a random identifier RevenueCat generates for this installation of the app, along with ordinary technical details such as the app and operating-system version, and — as with any internet request — your device's IP address. Nothing about your habits is ever included. It cannot be: the app never gives RevenueCat any of your data.
  • If you subscribe, the store handles the purchase and the payment: Apple on iPhone, Google Play on Android. Buying never gives Nanbit your card details, your Apple Account or Google account, your name or your email address. Each store's handling of your data is covered by its own policy: Apple's Privacy Policy and Google's Privacy Policy.
  • RevenueCat, acting on our behalf, checks the purchase with the store it was made in — Apple or Google Play — and tells the app whether the subscription is active. What is processed is your purchase history — which product was bought, when, and whether it is still active or in a trial — tied to that same random installation identifier. It is not your Apple Account or Google account, it is not linked to your name or email, and it is not linked to your Nanbit account if you have one. RevenueCat's handling of it is covered by RevenueCat's Privacy Policy.

This is used for two things: unlocking paid features and restoring your purchase on a new device, and aggregate subscription figures such as how many people subscribed in a month. It is not used for advertising, and it is not used to track you across other apps or websites.

3. Recovering from a crash

If Nanbit stops unexpectedly, the next launch can repair the app by fetching a corrected version of its own code from Expo's update service. It does not check at any other time — not on a normal launch, and not while the app is running.

That request carries the platform, the app version, a random identifier for this installation, and the text of the error that caused the crash: the error message and the programmer-facing trace of where in Nanbit's own code it happened, cut off after about a thousand characters. This is what makes it possible to tell whether a fix exists for the problem you actually hit. It is not a report about you or your habits, and there is no crash-reporting service or dashboard behind it — but it is error text leaving the device, and we would rather say so than describe the request as carrying less than it does. Expo's handling of it is covered by Expo's Privacy Policy.

The random identifier here is generated by the app. It is not your Apple Account or Google account, not an advertising identifier, and not connected to any account.

4. Links you tap

The support, privacy and terms links open your browser at an ordinary web address. Nothing about you or your habits is attached; your browser behaves as it always does.

5. Your phone's own backup, which Nanbit does not send

If your phone backs itself up — iCloud Backup on iPhone, Google's backup on Android — that backup can contain Nanbit's database: your habits, completions and settings, and the deleted records described above. On iPhone it may also contain the widget's copy; whether iCloud Backup includes it has not been checked, so this policy assumes it can. Nanbit does not send any of it and cannot read it. It goes to your own Apple or Google account under your phone's backup settings, and Apple's or Google's privacy policy covers it rather than this one.

What is known differs by platform. On iPhone, Apple documents that iCloud Backup includes the data of apps you have downloaded, and Nanbit keeps its database where that applies. On Android it is expected rather than confirmed: Nanbit uses Android's standard backup settings, which include the app's own storage, but no restore has been tested yet.

So deleting Nanbit clears its database from the phone, and Reset all data, signing out and Delete account each clear the database inside it and replace the widget's copy — but none of them reaches a backup the phone has already made. What happens to that backup is up to your phone's backup settings, not to Nanbit.

What Nanbit does not do

  • No analytics about how you use the app. Nothing measures which screens you open, which habits you keep, or how often you come back. The only aggregate figures that exist anywhere are RevenueCat's subscription counts, described above.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No tracking, as Apple defines it: nothing about you is linked to data from other companies' apps or websites, for any purpose.
  • No crash-reporting service. The one exception is the update request described in section 3, which carries the last error's text.
  • No account unless you choose one. Nothing in Nanbit asks you to sign in.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data.
  • No location, contacts, photos, microphone, camera or health data. Nanbit never asks for them because it never uses them.

Notifications

Reminders are scheduled by your phone, locally, from the habits already on your device. There is no push server and no push token: nothing about a reminder leaves the phone, and Nanbit cannot send you anything you did not schedule yourself. Because a reminder names the habit it is for, a habit's name can appear on your lock screen — that is your phone displaying it, not Nanbit sending it anywhere. You can turn notifications off at any time in the app or in your phone's settings.

On Android, reminders rely on two permissions. One lets Nanbit post notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS); Android 13 and later ask you for it. The other lets Nanbit start when the phone does (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED), so that reminders you have already scheduled are set again after a restart. Neither gives Nanbit access to anything else on your phone.

Your choices and controls

  • See what Nanbit holds — your habits, completions and settings are all visible in the app, and the backup is a copy of exactly those. For a copy of what your account holds, write to us.
  • Hide individual records — deleting a habit removes it and its completions from everywhere in the app, and from the next backup if you are signed in. As described above, the underlying rows remain in the database file on your device until you reset, sign out, or remove the app.
  • Delete your account — You → Delete account, described in section 1. It is only shown while you are signed in.
  • Delete everything on the phone — when you are not signed in, You → About Nanbit → Reset all data erases the database, deleted records included. It leaves behind a little of Nanbit's own diagnostic bookkeeping, and it does not withdraw the notification permission you gave your phone, so it stops just short of a fresh install. Removing the app clears more, apart from a sign-in left in the Keychain, described above. Neither reaches a backup your phone has already made (section 5).
  • Stop using Sign in with Apple with Nanbit — open the Settings app, tap your name, tap Sign in with Apple, choose Nanbit, then tap Delete. Nanbit notices and signs out on this phone. Your habits stay on the phone, and your account stays until you delete it.
  • Manage or cancel a subscription — on iPhone, open the Settings app, tap your name, then tap Subscriptions; on Android, go to subscriptions in Google Play. Cancelling never deletes anything you have tracked.
  • Ask us to delete purchase records — the subscription records held by RevenueCat are keyed to a random installation identifier. If you want them erased, write to delorinodenver@gmail.com and we will action it. You may need to tell us roughly when and what you purchased, since we have no other way to find you.

How to reach us about your data

Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com. That address reaches Denver Delorino, who makes Nanbit, and it is the only route — there is no support desk and no ticketing system behind it.

If you have an account, tell us the email address Apple gave Nanbit so we can find it. If you chose Hide My Email, that is the forwarding address, which you can see under Sign in with Apple in your iPhone's Settings. If you have never signed in, Nanbit's server holds nothing of yours, so the only records we can look for are purchase records: tell us roughly what you bought and when. A reply normally takes a few days.

If you are in the UK or the EU, you have rights of access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection and portability over personal data. They apply to your account and its backup, if you have one, and to the purchase records described above. You can delete your account yourself, in the app, at any time. For anything else, write to us.

How long data is kept

Data on your device is kept until you delete it, sign out, or remove the app; a copy in your phone's own backup is kept under your backup settings (section 5). Records you delete inside the app are hidden immediately and stay in the database file until you reset, sign out, or remove the app.

Your account and its backup are kept until you delete the account. Each save replaces the last one, so there is only ever one backup, and no older versions. The sign-in records go when the account does. Supabase says it keeps its request logs for up to 7 days; it does not say how long it keeps its backup copies or its security records (section 1). Purchase records are kept by the store you bought through — Apple or Google — and by RevenueCat, for as long as needed to support restores, refunds and financial record-keeping.

Children

Nanbit is not directed at children under 13. It never asks for an age. Signing in is optional. Without it, Nanbit collects no name, no email and no contact details, and the only information it sends off the device is a random per-installation identifier, subscription status, and — after a crash — the text of the error. If you sign in, your account also holds the email address Apple gives Nanbit, and the backup holds the display name on the You tab (section 1).

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. That date is the whole record. There is no notice: Nanbit does not send email, even to people who have an account, and promising a notice we would not send would be worse than saying so.
