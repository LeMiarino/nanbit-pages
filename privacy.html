Nanbit Privacy Policy

Effective 13 September 2026. Nanbit is made by Denver Delorino, an individual developer.

Nanbit is a habit tracker. There is no account and no Nanbit server. This policy explains what is kept on your phone, the few things that leave it, and how to delete everything.

The short version

  • Your habits, your completions and your settings are kept on your phone. Nanbit never sends them anywhere. If your phone backs itself up, that backup can include them — see section 4 below.
  • There is no account and no sign-in, and no Nanbit server for anything to go to.
  • Nanbit has no analytics about how you use the app, no advertising, no tracking, and no crash-reporting service.
  • Nanbit uses a service called RevenueCat to manage subscriptions. In a version of Nanbit configured to sell them, a small request goes to RevenueCat when Nanbit opens, whether or not you ever subscribe. It identifies this installation and this phone, and carries nothing about your habits. A version that is not configured to sell subscriptions does not contact RevenueCat at all.
  • If you buy a Nanbit subscription, the store you buy it through processes the payment: Apple on iPhone, Google Play on Android.
  • If Nanbit crashes while it is starting up, it may ask Expo's update service for a corrected version of its own code, and that request includes the error message. It does not check at any other time.
  • Nanbit does not sell or share your data.

What Nanbit stores on your phone

All of this lives in a database inside the app's own storage, which only Nanbit can read:

  • Habits — the name, icon, colour, schedule, daily goal and reminder time you set.
  • Completions — which habit you marked done, on which day, and how many times.
  • Settings — your display name, appearance choice, which day your week starts on, the streak grace-day switch, your reminder preferences, and some bookkeeping such as whether you have finished the introduction. The display name is whatever you type; Nanbit never asks for your real name.
  • A summary cache — daily totals worked out from the above, kept so the charts are fast. It contains nothing new.
  • Records you have deleted. When you delete a habit or clear a day, Nanbit marks the record as deleted and hides it everywhere in the app, but the row stays in the database file. Nanbit never sends it anywhere, though a backup your phone makes copies the database whole, deleted records included. Reset all data removes it along with everything else.

A copy for the widget, on iPhone. Nanbit also keeps a small copy for its home-screen widget, even if you never add the widget. It is outside the database, in storage that only Nanbit and its widget can read. It says which habits are due and which are done, today and for the next week, as marks and totals, without the habits' names. Reset all data replaces it with one that holds no habits. If that replacement fails, the widget can go on showing the last week it was given until Nanbit next updates it.

Deleting Nanbit deletes all of this from the phone, the widget's copy included. It does not reach a backup the phone has already made — see section 4.

What leaves your phone

Nanbit itself sends three things, and nothing else. A fourth is sent by your phone rather than by Nanbit: its own backup, described last.

1. Subscriptions, and the app knowing whether you have one

Nanbit uses RevenueCat to tell whether a subscription is active. Whether it contacts RevenueCat at all is settled when a version of the app is built: a version configured to sell subscriptions contacts it, and a version that is not does not contact it at all — not at launch, not when you open the subscription screen, not at any other time.

  • When you open Nanbit, and when you return to it, the app asks RevenueCat what this installation is entitled to. This happens whether or not you have ever subscribed, and whether or not you have ever opened the subscription screen. What is sent is a random identifier RevenueCat generates for this installation of the app, the identifier Apple gives this phone for apps from Nanbit's developer, and ordinary technical details such as the app and operating-system version, the phone model, your preferred languages and your App Store country — and, as with any internet request, your device's IP address. Nothing about your habits is ever included. It cannot be: the app never gives RevenueCat any of your data.
  • If you subscribe, the store handles the purchase and the payment: Apple on iPhone, Google Play on Android. Buying never gives Nanbit your card details, your Apple Account or Google account, your name or your email address. Each store's handling of your data is covered by its own policy: Apple's Privacy Policy and Google's Privacy Policy.
  • RevenueCat, acting on our behalf, checks the purchase with the store it was made in — Apple or Google Play — and tells the app whether the subscription is active. What is processed is your purchase history — which product was bought, when, and whether it is still active or in a trial — tied to those same identifiers. It is not your Apple Account or Google account, and it is not linked to your name or email. RevenueCat's handling of it is covered by RevenueCat's Privacy Policy.

This is used for two things: unlocking paid features and restoring your purchase on a new device, and aggregate subscription figures such as how many people subscribed in a month. It is not used for advertising, and it is not used to track you across other apps or websites.

2. Recovering from a crash

If Nanbit stops unexpectedly while it is starting up — from the moment it opens until a few seconds after its first screen appears — it can ask Expo's update service for a corrected version of its own code before it closes. It does not check at any other time: not on a normal launch, not while the app is running, and not after a crash later on.

That request carries the platform, the app version, a random identifier for this installation, and the error message: what went wrong in Nanbit's code and, if it went wrong while a screen was being drawn, which parts of Nanbit's code were drawing it, cut off after about a thousand characters. It is not a report about you or your habits — the messages Nanbit's code writes never include a habit, a day you ticked or a setting — and there is no crash-reporting service or dashboard behind it. But it is error text leaving the phone, and we would rather say so than describe the request as carrying less than it does. Expo's handling of it is covered by Expo's Privacy Policy.

The random identifier here is generated by the app. It is not your Apple Account or Google account, and not an advertising identifier.

3. Links you tap

The support, privacy and terms links open your browser at an ordinary web address. Nothing about you or your habits is attached; your browser behaves as it always does.

4. Your phone's own backup, which Nanbit does not send

If your phone backs itself up — iCloud Backup on iPhone, Google's backup on Android — that backup can contain Nanbit's database: your habits, completions and settings, and the deleted records described above. On iPhone it may also contain the widget's copy; whether iCloud Backup includes it has not been checked, so this policy assumes it can. Nanbit does not send any of it and cannot read it. It goes to your own Apple or Google account under your phone's backup settings, and Apple's or Google's privacy policy covers it rather than this one.

What is known differs by platform. On iPhone, Apple documents that iCloud Backup includes the data of apps you have downloaded, and nothing in Nanbit keeps its database out of it. On Android it is expected rather than confirmed: Nanbit uses Android's standard backup settings, which include the app's own storage, but no restore has been tested yet.

So deleting Nanbit clears it from the phone, and Reset all data clears the database inside it and replaces the widget's copy — but neither reaches a backup the phone has already made. What happens to that backup is up to your phone's backup settings, not to Nanbit.

Moving to a new iPhone

Set up the new iPhone from your old one, or from its iCloud backup, and your habits come along with your other apps' data. Apple's backup includes the data of apps you have downloaded, and Nanbit keeps nothing out of it or out of a direct transfer. We have not yet watched this happen with Nanbit, so check your habits on the new phone before you erase the old one.

What Nanbit does not do

  • No analytics about how you use the app. Nothing measures which screens you open, which habits you keep, or how often you come back. The only aggregate figures that exist anywhere are RevenueCat's subscription counts, described above.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No tracking, as Apple defines it: nothing about you is linked to data from other companies' apps or websites, for any purpose.
  • No crash-reporting service. The one exception is the update request described in section 2, which carries the error's text.
  • No account and no sign-in. Nothing in Nanbit asks who you are.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data.
  • No location, contacts, photos, microphone, camera or health data. Nanbit never asks for them because it never uses them.

Notifications

Reminders are scheduled by your phone, locally, from the habits already on your device. There is no push server and no push token: nothing about a reminder leaves the phone, and Nanbit cannot send you anything you did not schedule yourself. Because a reminder names the habit it is for, a habit's name can appear on your lock screen — that is your phone displaying it, not Nanbit sending it anywhere. You can turn notifications off at any time in the app or in your phone's settings.

On Android, reminders rely on two permissions. One lets Nanbit post notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS); Android 13 and later ask you for it. The other lets Nanbit start when the phone does (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED), so that reminders you have already scheduled are set again after a restart. Neither gives Nanbit access to anything else on your phone.

Your choices and controls

  • See what Nanbit holds — your habits, completions and settings are all visible in the app. There is no other copy of them anywhere Nanbit can reach.
  • Hide individual records — deleting a habit removes it and its completions from everywhere in the app. As described above, the underlying rows remain in the database file on your phone until you reset or remove the app.
  • Delete everything on the phone — You → About Nanbit → Reset all data erases the database, deleted records included, and empties the widget's copy. It leaves behind a little of Nanbit's own diagnostic bookkeeping, and it does not withdraw the notification permission you gave your phone, so it stops just short of a fresh install. Removing the app clears more. Neither reaches a backup your phone has already made (section 4).
  • Manage or cancel a subscription — on iPhone, open the Settings app, tap your name, then tap Subscriptions; on Android, go to subscriptions in Google Play. Cancelling never deletes anything you have tracked.
  • Ask us to delete purchase records — the subscription records held by RevenueCat are keyed to the identifiers described in section 1. If you want them erased, write to delorinodenver@gmail.com and we will action it. You may need to tell us roughly when and what you purchased, since we have no other way to find you.

How to reach us about your data

Write to delorinodenver@gmail.com. That address reaches Denver Delorino, who makes Nanbit, and it is the only route — there is no support desk and no ticketing system behind it.

Nanbit has no server, so nothing of your habits is held anywhere we can look. The only records we can look for are purchase records: tell us roughly what you bought and when. A reply normally takes a few days.

If you are in the UK or the EU, you have rights of access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection and portability over personal data. They apply to the purchase records described above. Your habits are on your phone, where you can see, change and delete them yourself. For anything else, write to us.

How long data is kept

Data on your phone is kept until you delete it, reset, or remove the app; a copy in your phone's own backup is kept under your backup settings (section 4). Records you delete inside the app are hidden immediately and stay in the database file until you reset or remove the app.

Purchase records are kept by the store you bought through — Apple or Google — and by RevenueCat, for as long as needed to support restores, refunds and financial record-keeping.

Children

Nanbit is not directed at children under 13. It never asks for an age, and it collects no name, no email and no contact details from anyone. The only information it sends off the phone is the identifiers and subscription status described in section 1 and — after a crash while it is starting up — the text of the error.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. That date is the whole record. There is no notice: Nanbit does not send email, and promising a notice we would not send would be worse than saying so.
